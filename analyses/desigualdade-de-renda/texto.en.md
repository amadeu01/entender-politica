## What the data show

### The 1980s: high and rising inequality

According to the old PNAD survey, the Gini of household income per capita was
{{ v("ipeadata.disoc_rdcg", "BRA", 1981) }} in 1981 and reached
{{ v("ipeadata.disoc_rdcg", "BRA", 1989) }} in 1989, the highest value in the series. It
was a decade of stagnant income and rising inflation: GDP per capita changed by
{{ pct("wdi.ny_gdp_pcap_kd", "BRA", 1980, 1990) }} between 1980 and 1990, and consumer
price inflation reached {{ v("wdi.fp_cpi_totl_zg", "BRA", 1989) }}% in 1989.

### The 1990s: less poverty, same inequality

Between 1993 and 1995, the period of the Real Plan, the poverty rate measured with Ipea's
regional lines went from {{ v("ipeadata.disoc_ppy", "BRA", 1993) }}% to
{{ v("ipeadata.disoc_ppy", "BRA", 1995) }}%. The Gini barely moved:
{{ v("ipeadata.disoc_rdcg", "BRA", 1993) }} in 1993 and
{{ v("ipeadata.disoc_rdcg", "BRA", 1995) }} in 1995. Barros, Henriques and Mendonça (2000)
described this as an "unacceptable stability" of inequality.

### 2001 to 2014: a sustained fall

The old PNAD Gini changed by {{ diff("ipeadata.disoc_rdcg", "BRA", 2001, 2014) }} between
2001 and 2014, from {{ v("ipeadata.disoc_rdcg", "BRA", 2001) }} to
{{ v("ipeadata.disoc_rdcg", "BRA", 2014) }}. Over the same period, the poverty rate with
regional lines went from {{ v("ipeadata.disoc_ppy", "BRA", 2003) }}% in 2003 to
{{ v("ipeadata.disoc_ppy", "BRA", 2014) }}% in 2014.

### Since 2012: the Continuous PNAD

IBGE's current official series starts at {{ v("ibge_sidra.gini_pnadc", "BRA", 2012) }} in
2012. After the 2015–2016 recession, when GDP per capita changed by
{{ pct("wdi.ny_gdp_pcap_kd", "BRA", 2014, 2016) }} in two years, the index rose to
{{ v("ibge_sidra.gini_pnadc", "BRA", 2018) }} in 2018. In 2020 it went to
{{ v("ibge_sidra.gini_pnadc", "BRA", 2020) }} and back to
{{ v("ibge_sidra.gini_pnadc", "BRA", 2021) }} in 2021; in those two years income comes from
the survey's 5th visit rather than the 1st, because of the pandemic. In 2024 the value was
{{ v("ibge_sidra.gini_pnadc", "BRA", 2024) }}. The chart shows each year's {{ lit("95%", "confidence level of the interval computed from IBGE's coefficient of variation; see the catalog") }} interval:
small differences between adjacent years may not be statistically significant.

### Brazil and its neighbors

The fall of the 2000s was not unique to Brazil. Between 2002 and 2014, on the World Bank
Gini ({{ lit("0 to 100", "index scale published by the World Bank") }} scale), Brazil changed by {{ diff("wdi.si_pov_gini", "BRA", 2002, 2014) }},
Mexico by {{ diff("wdi.si_pov_gini", "MEX", 2002, 2014) }} and Colombia by
{{ diff("wdi.si_pov_gini", "COL", 2002, 2014) }}; urban Argentina changed by
{{ diff("wdi.si_pov_gini", "ARG", 2003, 2014) }} between 2003 and 2014. When several
countries move together, factors common to the region probably
matter, alongside national policies.
